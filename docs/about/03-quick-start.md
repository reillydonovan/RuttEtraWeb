## Quick Start

The shortest path to your first scanline mesh. Each step is one short action.

1. On the boot overlay, choose a source: **WEBCAM**, **SCREEN**, **FILE**, or **STREAM URL** — or click **continue without video** to use the Pattern Generator alone. Grant camera access if prompted. If the site operator has deployed a sample video, a **Load Sample Video** button is also available in Source → Video File.
2. Open the **Presets** panel and click **Phosphor Green** to load a starting look.
3. Drag inside the viewport — your cursor rotates the camera around the scanline mesh.
4. Open the **DCU** panel and adjust **Z Push**. The scanlines lift and fall as you turn it.
5. Open **Modulation** and add a patch: Source = `LFO (Sine)`, Target = `Z Push`, Amount = `0.5`. Watch the scanlines pulse.
6. Open **Record → Start Recording**. Stop when ready. The file downloads to your machine.

That's the whole synth. Everything below is the rest of what it can do.

**The menu bar** — a traditional app menu strip along the top (desktop only). **File** holds project New/Open/Save/Save As, the recent-projects list, and whole-workspace backup (Export/Import Everything) — the same operations as the Project panel and the Ctrl+N/O/S shortcuts, one implementation behind all three. **Edit** is timeline undo/redo; **View** switches skins, HUD, fullscreen, panel/timeline docking, and **Text Size**; **Window** pops the controls or output into separate windows and manages workspace regions (below); **Help** jumps straight to any Help tab, opens this manual, or shows the build's About box. The old always-visible ABOUT corner link moved into the Help menu.

**Text Size** (`View ▸ Text Size`, or `Ctrl+=` / `Ctrl+-` / `Ctrl+0`) — a legibility preference for anyone who finds the default hard to read. It scales every window's controls and fonts together and is remembered on this machine (like the skin choice, not in your presets or projects) — the default stays exactly as-is for everyone who never touches it.

**Workspace windows & tabs** — the interface is four windows (**left**, **center**, **right**, **bottom**), and each holds a strip of **tabs**, exactly like Unity or Blender. Click a tab to switch what the window shows (**RENDERER**, **CONTROLS**, **TIMELINE**, **PATCH BAY**); **drag a tab** onto another window's tab strip to move it there — a thin caret nudges between tabs to show exactly where it will land, the strip highlights, and while you drag, the *empty* window nearest your pointer reveals itself as a labeled drop strip (one at a time, proximity-based); drag within a strip to **reorder**; **✕ closes a tab** (any window's **[+]** reopens it). **[+] always just adds a tab** — RENDERER and PATCH BAY get a fresh instance per window, while CONTROLS and TIMELINE (one live panel each) simply follow your most recent click: the window you left shows a small "click the tab to bring it over" placeholder. Duplicate tabs are fully live: two TIMELINE tabs in different windows both play; two CONTROLS panels both edit (every instance drives the same underlying settings, so they always agree). Moving a view never disturbs another window, and a window with no tabs disappears until you give it one — while dragging, empty windows reveal themselves near your pointer on all four edges (top and bottom drops also pick the bottom window's dock edge) plus the center. The renderer moves like any view — away from the center it becomes a live mirror while whatever you promoted takes the center stage. Side and bottom windows also have **⤢ maximize** (double-click the bar works too) and **– collapse**. **Window ▸ Layout** has the preset family — `Default (4-up)` (Patch Bay left, Renderer center, Controls right, Timeline bottom), `Classic`, `Tall`, `Wide`, `Performance`, `Patch Bay` — plus **Reset Layout**, the escape hatch back to Default from any arrangement you regret. Window arrangements save with your project; phones/tablets keep the standard mobile layout regardless.

**The Patch Bay** — a view that turns the whole instrument into a hardware faceplate — and a node editor: **scroll-wheel to zoom** (or the −/100%/+ buttons in the SOURCES bar), **click-drag any empty background to pan** around the board, **drag any module by its header** to reorder it within the stacks (an insertion line shows where it lands) or drop it out past them to arrange nodes freely, Max/MSP style, with magnetic edge-snapping; **TIDY** snaps everything back. A free arrangement survives being squeezed into a narrow window, and gets extra room around its edges so the outermost node's far side is always reachable while panning (the default stacked layout already fills the window, so it skips that padding). It's still the same faceplate underneath: four **FUNCTION GENERATOR** modules (the Classic mod slots) with SRC IN and OUT jacks, a **VIDEO SOURCE** module (WEBCAM / SCREEN / FILE, the same switch as the Source panel), a **SOURCES** field of signal jacks (waveforms, audio, beat, joystick), and every modulatable parameter as a labeled jack **with a rotary knob and its real current value in an editable field right underneath** (drag the knob vertically, Shift for fine control, exactly like the hardware skin's pots) — every field group (Position & Size, Depth, Beam, Rotation, Scan, Linearity, Luminance, Colorizer/Post-FX, Camera, Pattern, Audio Analyzer, Auxiliary), so you can run the whole synth from the bay without the Controls tab open at all; only the CV OUT jacks (virtual, not real settings) skip the knob and value field. While modulation drives a param, an **amber needle** rides its knob and an amber `▸ value` readout shows the live figure — the same orange live-value language as the Controls tab. And matching the real RE-4's faceplate, **every param row has an OUT jack too**: drag Height's OUT onto Depth's IN and the param's live value becomes a signal feeding the other param (a reusable *Param Out* node appears in the GRAPH ENGINE section, reading one frame behind — the same clean delay as a feedback loop). Drag a generator's OUT to any param jack to route it (or drag from the param jack back); drop a plugged cable in space to unplug; drag a SOURCES jack into a SRC IN to pick that generator's signal. It's the same Classic matrix you already know — the cables *are* the slot Source/Target settings, so the Modulation panel's dropdowns update as you patch (and vice versa). A **GRAPH ENGINE** section shows one module per graph node in the patch (labeled LFO, Audio Envelope, Sync, etc. — not one generic chip) — every node, not just ones doing something yet; a node with no connections anywhere is dimmed so it reads as a placeholder, not a signal in use. Each module is a full peer of the FUNCTION GENERATOR modules: drag its header to reorder or break it into free layout, same as any other module. Its cyan param cables are draggable too: grab a bound param's jack and drop it on a different param to re-route that node's output there, or drop it in empty space to unplug it (the binding stays in the graph, just detached, so you can re-plug it later); a node feeding several params at once (fan-out) only moves the one cable you grab, the others hold. **Composite two video sources into the render output.** A second, fully independent **VIDEO SOURCE B** module joins Source A (same WEBCAM/SCREEN/FILE picker and live preview), and a **COMPOSITE** module takes two video inputs plus the same 8 blend modes the Pattern Generator already offers (Add, Subtract, Multiply, Screen, Difference, Mask, Crossfade, B-only) and a mix control. Drag Source A's OUT jack and Source B's OUT jack onto Composite's A/B inputs, then Composite's OUT onto the new **RENDERER** module's IN jack, and the blend becomes what the mesh actually displaces — a file layered over a webcam, a screen-share crossfaded with a camera. RENDERER IN's unplugged state shows no cable at all (like a real Eurorack normalled jack) because it silently defaults straight to Source A — nothing about existing sessions changes until you actually drag a connection there.

Some nodes also take an input from ANOTHER node instead of just producing an output (Ramp's trigger, Noise's clock) — those render as amber IN jacks, wireable straight from another node's OUT jack (drag either end), no need to open the Graph editor's canvas just for that. Drag a node's OUT jack straight onto a faceplate param too — say an LFO onto H Position — and it patches a live binding the same way a Classic slot's cable does; a node's own OUT jack stays fixed as a source either way (grabbing it only ever adds a new connection, never retargets or removes one). Node params render as real controls on the module — rotary knobs for numbers, dropdowns for choices — and waveform pickers like the LFO's *shape* get their own small jack: **drag TRI from the SOURCES bus onto it for a triangle wave** (the cable stays plugged as a picture of the current shape). Want to SEE a signal? Add a **Scope** node and splice it into any cable — it passes the signal through unchanged and draws the live waveform on its module, phosphor-style. In free layout, dropped modules push themselves apart so nodes never end up overlapping, and loading a sample or library patch while you have a patch going asks before replacing it (Cancel keeps yours). **Right-click any empty board background** for a categorized Add Node menu (the same node list the Graph editor's palette has, Blender/Max-MSP style) — the new module **spawns free-floating right where you clicked**, never overlapping another module, ready to wire up without leaving the bay; it also shows up as a normal row in Controls ▸ Modulation's Beginner view (switching out of Classic mode automatically if needed), so the two surfaces always agree on what the patch is — and any patching gesture wakes the graph engine automatically, so a fresh wire is always live, never silent. Each module also shows the node's own knobs — shape, rate, and every other param it has, plus a mute LED — right on the faceplate, between its IN and OUT jacks, no need to open the Graph editor's inspector for a quick tweak. The VIDEO SOURCE module has an expandable **live preview** of the incoming feed, and a **RENDER PREVIEW** module monitors the final output the same way (both collapsed, and free while collapsed). New to the bay? **Help ▸ Patch Bay Tutorial** is a second guided walkthrough teaching spawning, knobs, wiring, and the Scope, with try-it buttons. Open the bay from a region dropdown or `Window ▸ Layout ▸ Patch Bay`.

**Pop any window out to another display** — every window bar has a **⧉** button beside maximize/collapse that opens that window's active view as its own OS window: Patch Bay and Timeline get a fresh, fully-live duplicate instance (everything stays in sync through the shared stores — closing the popup loses nothing); Controls and the Renderer use their dedicated popouts. Perfect for a two-monitor rig: output on the projector, bay on the laptop, timeline on a side screen.

**Remote control via MCP** — the whole synth is drivable from an MCP client (Claude Desktop, Claude Code, ...): run the bridge in `tools/mcp-server/`, then connect from **I/O ▸ MCP** (opt-in, nothing connects by itself). The MCP tools cover every modulatable parameter, every menu command, the whole graph patch (read, replace, add nodes), settings reads, and PNG screenshots of the output — see the bridge's README for setup.

**Slider tricks** — every numeric control accepts typed input in the box beside the slider; double-click (or double-tap) resets to default; arrow keys nudge by one step. The small **▾ at the top-left of every slider** opens its value & range menu: set a **custom range** (a min/max clamp — widen a slider up to 5× its stock span past either bound for extra headroom, or narrow it into a fine-tuning vernier), reset the range, or reset the value. **Right-clicking the slider** (or tapping its readout on touch) opens the same precise-entry panel directly. Custom ranges persist with your project; the ▾ and number box glow while one is active.

**Hover tooltips** — hold the mouse still over any parameter row or section header for about half a second and a short description pops up above it, explaining what that control does. Move the mouse away and it disappears immediately. This is the same text that drives the in-app Help's Controls tab and this manual's Param Reference — one description, everywhere it's needed. Mouse only (there's no hover state to speak of on touch); nothing to turn on or off.

**Panel skins** — the **skin picker** in the panel title bar (CRT / HW / RACK) switches between three looks; **fresh sessions start on RACK**. **CRT** is the flat original. **HW** swaps every control for skeuomorphic hardware: rotary knobs (drag vertically to turn; hold `Shift` for fine control; double-click to reset), bat switches with LED lenses, and LCD readouts (click one for precise entry, same as right-clicking). **RACK** adds full modular chrome on top — sections become rack modules with faceplate screws, every modulatable knob grows a 3.5mm input jack, and your actual modulation routings render as live patch cables: green cables from the Classic matrix's slot output jacks (drag in either direction — from a slot's output jack to a param's input jack, or from an empty input jack back to a slot; drop a plugged cable in space to unplug — this edits the slot's Target exactly like the dropdown does), cyan cables for Graph-engine bindings (edit those in the graph editor). A cable into a collapsed section plugs into that section's header, so nothing vanishes off-screen. Same parameters, same values in every skin — only the faces change, and every slider trick above still works. The skin choice is remembered on this machine, not in your presets or projects, and touch layouts keep their touch-friendly controls regardless.

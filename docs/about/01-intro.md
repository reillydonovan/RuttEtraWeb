# Rutt/Etra — Manual

Rutt/Etra is a browser-based recreation of the analog video synthesizer designed by Steve Rutt and Bill Etra at Rutt Electrophysics, New York, in 1973. Their machine took an incoming video signal — a camera, a tape, a test pattern — and displaced each scanline along the Z axis in proportion to the brightness of each pixel. The result was a wireframe terrain made of TV. It became the foundation visual instrument of a generation of video artists: Nam June Paik, Steina Vasulka, Bill Viola, Gary Hill.

This synth keeps faith with the original. Every knob from the Rutt Electrophysics control set is here. The scanlines, the depth, the focus, the rotation, the linearity — same instrument, modern substrate. Then it goes further: full modular-style modulation with voltage control, video itself as a control signal, audio-reactive everything, MIDI sync, OSC, CV input and output for connecting to real hardware modulars. The browser is the chassis. Your video is the source. Whatever you can patch is what it does.

This page is the long-form manual. The in-app **Help** panel is its short-form sibling — a quick-reference dock you read while you work. Everything in there is here, in more detail, with worked examples and reference tables you can print.

> Original hardware design: Steve Rutt & Bill Etra, Rutt Electrophysics, 1973. Control set documentation sourced from the Rutt/Etra Video Synthesizer brochure in the [Vasulka archive](https://vasulka.org/archive/Artists5/Rutt-E/VidSynthCont.pdf).
